# Q1. The daily / weekly rhythm of a discretionary trader or small fund

## 1.1 The overall shape of the day

The strongest structural finding across all account types is that **the trading day has three distinct phases — a long preparation block before the open, a live session, and a post-close processing block — and that for desks in continuous markets (FX, rates, credit, ADRs) the day is effectively continuous rather than bell-bounded.**

The clearest generic timetable I found comes from Financial Edge Training's trading-analyst walkthrough, which is explicitly modelled on a US sell-side desk with London already open [SECONDARY - training provider desk description]:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 6:00–7:00am | "Arriving at the Office and Pre-Desk Prep" — "Many traders log in from home around 6:00am to review overnight news and market moves, central bank commentary, and client emails." ([fe.training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/careers-in-finance/day-in-the-life-of-trading-analyst/)) |
| 6:30–7:00am | "Pre-Market Routine" — "at their desks assessing risk levels, updating pricing grids, and sending early notes to sales flagging potential themes for the session." |
| 7:00–8:00am | "Morning Meeting" — "The global morning meeting sets expectations for the session. Research, sales, and trading teams dial in from multiple offices." |
| 8:00–9:30am | "Preparing for the Market Open" — opening books, auctions, order imbalances, bid/offer runs, vol surfaces |
| 9:30am | "Market Opens (for Equities)" — "triggering the fastest pace of the day for equity and equity-derivatives desks" |
| Intraday | "Eating lunch at the desk to avoid missing catalysts" — "Even during quieter moments, traders monitor P&L, order books, and market signals." |
| 4:00–4:30pm | "Market Close and Reconciliation" — "Trades are matched and allocated. Discrepancies are flagged to back office." |
| 5:30pm onward | "Risk Review and Wrap-Up" — "Most traders wrap up around 5:30–6:00pm" |

For a **continuous-market** desk the same source notes the open is not the pivot: "FX, Rates, and Credit trade continuously and do not depend on a single opening bell" and "Rates may see bursts of activity around major 8:30am economic releases rather than the equity open" ([fe.training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/careers-in-finance/day-in-the-life-of-trading-analyst/)).

**Brett Steenbarger** — the trading psychologist whose blog was a priority source — describes his own structure as **preparation the night before, execution in the morning**, in the most recent routine post I could retrieve:

> "The evening before, I've updated my market research and formulated tentative ideas and plans for the trading day. I quickly review market activity during the overnight hours (in Asia and Europe) and, if necessary, do a quick update of my ideas and plans… This is also when I set goals for the day and enter them into my daily calendar."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2019](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2019/05/trading-psychology-techniques-4.html))

His routine begins "between 4 and 5 AM EST" and contains **eight sequenced blocks**, of which only two are market-facing: (1) prayer, (2) cats, (3) "Quick Market Update and Look at Emails," (4) exercise, (5) prayer/meditation/reading, (6) family time, (7) "Following Markets — Here is where I will dig in, update my research, and revise my ideas for the day's trading," and (8) "Reassessing" during the session ([traderfeed.blogspot.com](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2019/05/trading-psychology-techniques-4.html)).

He also makes the process claim explicit: "Your preparation creates your trading psychology. Confidence cannot be created out of thin air; it has to be earned" and "By the end of the week, the prepared trader has five days of experience. The trader who is not practicing has one day of experience repeated five times" [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2016](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2016/12/your-preparation-creates-your-trading.html)).

In 2010 he described the actual content of that pre-open block for himself: "My trading day begins early in the morning with an assessment of overseas markets, an advance look at economic reports due for the day, a review of the prior day's market indicators, and a view of overnight Globex trading. Out of that overview comes my initial game plan for the day" [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2010](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2010/01/formulating-initial-game-plan-for-day.html)). He frames pre-open questions as three checks: baggage, preparation ("Have I identified significant price levels for the day?"), and "What am I working on?" [PRIMARY] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2009](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2009/05/three-questions-for-start-of-trading.html)).

### Prop desk: SMB Capital — the intraday clock (2009 reported account) — [SECONDARY - reported desk walkthrough]
This is the **only source I found that assigns clock times to intraday phases on a discretionary equities prop desk**:
> "Every morning at **8:30** there is a meeting where the traders discuss their best ideas and look at support/resistance levels. The trader said between **9:30-11:00 AM, the traders need to turn off all distractions and focus on trading**. At **11:00 AM, it's time to review the trades you made earlier in the day**."
> **11:00 AM–3:00 PM:** "Share Intraday Alerts / Review your important trades from the A.M / Talk with other traders who traded your stock/trading ideas / Go have lunch / Watch for intraday support & resistance levels / **SMB mid-day meeting** / **Write in your trading journal**"
> "So the most active trading occurs between **9:30 AM till 11 AM and in the last hour of trading**. The SMB Capital traders use the **11 AM to 3PM** to look for levels/resistance, volume gainers, and different filters in order to have an edge in the last hour of trading."
Source: [Benzinga, Sept 2009](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/benzinga-staff/2009/9/13/smb-capital-outlines-its-daily-trading-strategy) [SECONDARY - trade press reporting a desk walkthrough by SMB]

**Implication for Q2 (my arithmetic):** on this desk, of a 9:30am–4:00pm session (6.5 hours), only about **2 hours — 9:30–11:00 plus the final hour — are "most active" trading time, i.e. ~30% of the session.** The middle 11:00am–3:00pm block is explicitly reallocated to review, journaling, a mid-day meeting, and *preparation for the close* rather than to continuous price-watching.

### Prop desk: SMB Capital, midtown Manhattan (Steven Spencer, co-founder) — [PRIMARY - named practitioner]
Spencer publishes his desk's pre-open sequence in full. The 2018 version:

> "Every morning when I arrive at SMB's office in midtown Manhattan I have a pre-market routine. The routine serves two purposes: 1) To get me in the proper frame of mind to attack the markets and 2) Ensure that I'm in the best risk/reward stocks and setups."
> Steps: "Login to trading platform / Open charting software and link to trading platform / Open SMB Trading Tools / Open SMB Scanner (finds Stocks In Play) / Open SMB Game Plan Template (where I input ideas) / Sort Scanner by pre-market volume / Read news of stocks that have unusual pre-market trading activity / **Enter 3-5 ideas in Game Plan Template** / **Enter 2nd Day Plays into Game Plan Template** / **Discuss market and ideas in AM meeting (15-20 minutes)** / Enter alerts and trading scripts for all trading ideas."

Note the only hard duration in the entire routine: **the AM meeting is 15–20 minutes** [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([smbtraining.com](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/why-i-game-plan-every-day)).

An earlier and more granular version of the same routine (18 numbered steps, published by Steenbarger) adds: fill water jug, restart computer, open platform/options platform/charting, "Complete game play sheet with Stocks in Play ideas," "Enter top trading ideas into journal (important for later review process)," "Ideas must include entries/stops/targets/risk amount," "Conduct AM meeting," "Enter orders for Stocks in Play ideas discussed at AM meeting," then "Two minutes of breathing exercises," "Put on RT microphone," "Discuss top trading ideas and plan via audio." He notes the reason for pre-programming entries: "if you miss certain entries right at the open, it can impact your risk taking for the rest of the day" [PRIMARY - named practitioner via Steenbarger] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2015](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/03/best-practices-in-trading-organizing_2.html)).

Spencer's published rationale for the meeting: "The meeting is divided into three parts: Market Overview. Fresh News/Stocks in Play. Second Day Plays/Technical setups… For Steven, mental preparation makes successful execution in the heat of battle more likely" [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([moneyshow.com](https://www.moneyshow.com/articles/daytraders-41875/)).

Steenbarger separately confirmed the desk-level morning meeting from direct observation: "Morning meetings to prepare for the trading day in which multiple traders participate with ideas" [PRIMARY - named practitioner observation] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2009](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2009/10/my-visit-to-smb-capital.html)).

## 1.3 During the session

**Monitoring dominates, and it is largely boring.** The most candid practitioner description I found:

> "Most of it is uneventful. Long periods of monitoring, punctuated by short bursts requiring immediate decisions. The skill is staying alert during the quiet so you react well during the busy, which is why simulation and reaction drills feature so heavily in training."
> — [SECONDARY - training vendor desk description] ([tradermath.org](https://www.tradermath.org/courses/markets-trading-and-risk/the-daily-life-of-a-trader-at-firms-like-imc))

The same source describes the intraday loop as: "Watch the market and the flow arriving → Update your estimate of fair value → Adjust quotes to reflect it and to manage inventory → Hedge what you have accumulated," with the real decisions living in the exceptions ("A news headline arrives and you widen or pull quotes… Volatility rises and your spreads need to reflect it").

**Monitoring is not the same as watching prices.** The Citadel Securities ADR trader is explicit:
> "My team and I aren't watching every single ADR that we cover, but we're focused instead on how our system is trading in terms of the liquidity it's providing for investors and the prices it's quoting for the securities. My job is mostly about watching trades take place electronically and recalibrating where necessary."
> — [PRIMARY] ([efinancialcareers.co.uk](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/06/citadel-securities-trading-job))

His session has an internal rhythm: 9:30am open (adrenaline), 10:30am QR chat, 11:00am RFQ flow begins, **11:30am "European markets close and there's usually a lull,"** 11:45am lunch, 12:15pm "it's during the afternoon when we typically start to see some dislocations from our models… we start building much larger positions," 1:30pm QR meeting, 2:00pm "This part of the day is fast-paced and exciting," 4:00pm close.

The quant equity trader's session description emphasizes total attachment: "No lunch time for traders. You can't afford to be out of the desk if something happens… I always eat at the trading desk watching the market. Every moment away from the desk has to be covered by a backup trader" [PRIMARY] ([CQF](https://www.cqf.com/blog/day-life-quantitative-equity-trader)).

**Intraday work is mostly position management and exceptions, not new idea generation.** Financial Edge's intraday list: "Managing and reducing unwanted inventory / Staying within risk limits and VAR thresholds / Updating sales with axes and trade ideas / Negotiating prices in fast-moving markets / Resolving trade breaks with operations / Eating lunch at the desk" ([fe.training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/careers-in-finance/day-in-the-life-of-trading-analyst/)).

**Steenbarger's description of intraday behaviour is re-planning, not reacting:** "Although most of my trades are intraday, I don't want to trade reactively, jumping from one trade to another. I reassess my plans and scenarios based upon the outcomes of the initial trades I placed… Very often, the first trade is smaller, as a kind of feeler in the market, leading me to reassess and place more significant trades based on that learning" [PRIMARY] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2019/05/trading-psychology-techniques-4.html)).

The PM who trades Asia/Europe/US describes the session as **scheduling around market hours rather than a fixed shift**: "on days when I'm trading Asia I have a different routine - I wake up at 4am and trade from home for a few hours before walking into the office. I'll then have a few hours off in the middle of the day before trading the US in the evenings" [SECONDARY - anonymous first-person, pseudonymous] ([efinancialcareers.co.uk](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/finance/portfolio-manager-job-hedge-fund-lifestyle)).

| SMB Capital prop desk (2009) | 11:00am "it's time to review the trades you made earlier in the day"; 11:00am–3:00pm block includes "**Write in your trading journal**" and an "SMB mid-day meeting" | [Benzinga](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/benzinga-staff/2009/9/13/smb-capital-outlines-its-daily-trading-strategy) [SECONDARY] |
| Citadel equities analyst | 9:00pm "I put together my checklist for the next day and get ready for bed." | [efinancialcareers.co.uk](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/what-is-a-hedge-fund-analyst-a-day-in-the-life-of-an-equities-analyst-at-citadel) [PRIMARY] |

**Journaling is treated as an input to position sizing and as a deliberate-practice loop, not a diary.** Steenbarger's series places the journal first in the sequence ("Trading Psychology Techniques - 1: Keeping a Trading Journal") and specifies both the daily and the weekly/monthly cadence:

> "**Frequency** - Note that, in cognitive therapy, people keep journals daily and make multiple entries per day. They write in the journal as soon after significant events occur… By journaling often, the person becomes very aware of their thinking."
> "**Backward and Forward Looking** - The ideal journal entry notes something distinctive that was done right or something distinctive that needs improvement… Then the journal entry looks forward to identify a concrete goal based on the observation and a specific plan."
> "**Reviewing as Well as Viewing** - If the journal entry sets a goal and a plan for reaching that goal, the next entry should spend some time reviewing how well the goal was reached."
> "**Keeping it Doable** … **You might want to work on one main goal per week or month**, depending upon the frequency of your trading… you don't want to be setting different goals every day, never truly building changes into robust habit patterns."
> "**At SMB, for example, trading journals structured as daily report cards are routinely shared with mentors** to facilitate feedback and learning."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2019](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2019/05/trading-psychology-techniques-1-keeping.html))

He also catalogues the failure modes: journals that "focus on Reporting," "focus on Venting," or suffer "Narrowness of Focus" — "The journal ends up being more descriptive than prescriptive. The journal as a reporting tool is not necessarily a performance-building tool."

The SMB routine separately requires ideas to be journaled *before* the open with "entries/stops/targets/risk amount" attached [PRIMARY] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/03/best-practices-in-trading-organizing_2.html)).

**Steenbarger's "blocks of routines" framing** (quoting money manager @MPortfolios) is the clearest statement of how the close feeds the open:

> "I have an **end of day routine** that is investment management focused, where I perform a scan of all positions vs. their various 'by rule' stop levels to determine if any have triggered, if any have risen above maximum allocation levels, etc. and determine if replacement positions are indicated and what they will be. **This routine sets up the following morning's initial actions.**"
> Morning: "1. Checking notifications and scan prior day's transactions (dividends, inflows, withdrawals) to know what's on my plate from an administrative standpoint; and 2. Looking at macro spread charts of asset classes, regions, and market sectors for signs of underlying changes in the landscape."
> "I've found that a really helpful byproduct of this… is that it fosters calm, systematic decision making."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner via Steenbarger] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2015](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/03/best-practices-in-trading-organizing.html))

## 1.5 Weekly and monthly rhythm

**Evidence here is materially thinner than for the daily cycle.** What I could substantiate:

**Weekly review of the idea pipeline (prop desk).** Steven Spencer gives a specific weekly test:
> "If at the end of the week **less than 50% of my morning ideas didn't offer excellent risk/reward trades** I need to determine what went wrong: Has the market changed? Was my analysis faulty? Are only a certain subset of my trading ideas working currently? Conversely, has a certain type of setup been offering better risk/reward than others? Then this setup should receive more risk allocation and more of my attention."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([smbtraining.com](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/why-i-game-plan-every-day))

**Weekly cumulative practice effect (coaching).** "By the end of the week, the prepared trader has five days of experience. The trader who is not practicing has one day of experience repeated five times" [PRIMARY] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2016/12/your-preparation-creates-your-trading.html)).

**Weekly goal-setting cadence (coaching).** Steenbarger's journaling protocol explicitly puts the change-cycle at a week or a month, not a day: "**You might want to work on one main goal per week or month**, depending upon the frequency of your trading. You don't want journaling to become unduly burdensome, and you don't want to be setting different goals every day, never truly building changes into robust habit patterns" [PRIMARY - named practitioner] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2019/05/trading-psychology-techniques-1-keeping.html)).

**Weekly economic-calendar horizon inside the daily routine.** The mutual-fund PM's 9:30am block is explicitly weekly in scope: "We discuss the week's upcoming economic releases and how our portfolio is structured to react to certain data points" [PRIMARY - anonymised] ([legacy.vault.com](https://legacy.vault.com/investment-management_portfolio-manager_major-mutual-fund-firm/day-in-the-life-of)). The Point72 associate works to "weekly research assignments" and a weekly "investment professional debrief" [PRIMARY] ([efinancialcareers.hk](https://www.efinancialcareers.hk/news/what-does-a-hedge-fund-analyst-do)).

**Monthly / quarterly: allocator-facing reporting cadence is well documented; internal strategy-review cadence is not.**
> "Institutional investors expect a performance estimate within a few business days of month end, then a final, administrator-confirmed figure once the net asset value is struck. That two-stage cadence is standard and acceptable." The monthly pack "should carry performance, exposure, liquidity, investor flows and any material operational change"; the quarterly pack "adds attribution, risk analytics, counterparty and concentration detail, valuation commentary and a governance update."
> — [SECONDARY - fund-administration vendor content; describes the market standard, and the page itself is marketing-adjacent. Treat the *content list* as reliable, the *cadence* as a reported norm, not a measured statistic] ([cv5capital.io](https://www.cv5capital.io/insights/hedge-fund-investor-reporting-requirements))

**Weekly review at the retail/prop end** is widely published but almost always without sourcing, e.g. a "Fifteen-Minute Friday Ritual" weekly review [FOLKLORE - unnamed methodology page] ([tradelyser.com](https://www.tradelyser.com/methodology/weekly-review)), a "Weekly Trading Review Checklist (20 Minutes)" [FOLKLORE - SEO content] ([edgeflo.com](https://www.edgeflo.com/blog/weekly-trading-review-checklist)), and a coach's claim that weekend analysis is what buys weekday freedom [FOLKLORE - podcast marketing copy] ([podtail/Trading Coach Podcast](https://podtail.com/podcast/the-trading-coach-podcast/1344-how-much-screen-time-does-a-trader-really-nee/)). I would not cite these as evidence of professional practice.

## 1.6 How much of the day is preparation vs live session? (derived arithmetic)

No source I found states this ratio directly for discretionary traders, so the table below is **my own arithmetic from the hour-by-hour accounts above** — the underlying timings are sourced, the percentages are mine. Flagged accordingly.

| Desk | Day span | Pre-open prep | Live session | Post-close | Live session share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Citadel Securities ADR trader [PRIMARY timings] ([source](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/06/citadel-securities-trading-job)) | 6:45am–7:30pm (12h45m) | ~2h45m (incl. 30-min commute; ~1h15m of it is actual market work) | 9:30am–4:00pm = 6h30m | 4:00–7:30pm = 3h30m | **~51%** |
| Bank trading desk [SECONDARY timings] ([source](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/careers-in-finance/day-in-the-life-of-trading-analyst/)) | 6:00am–6:00pm (12h) | ~3h30m | 9:30am–4:00pm = 6h30m | 4:00–6:00pm = 2h | **~54%** |
| Quant equity trader (RBC GAM) [PRIMARY timings] ([source](https://www.cqf.com/blog/day-life-quantitative-equity-trader)) | 6:00am–~5:00pm (11h, excl. evening) | 7:00–9:30am = 2h30m | 9:30am–4:00pm = 6h30m | 4:00pm+ (unquantified) | **~59%** |
| SMB Capital prop desk [PRIMARY, partial] ([source](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/why-i-game-plan-every-day)) | ~7:30am–4:00pm+ | routine of 11 steps + 15–20 min AM meeting (duration unstated) | 9:30am–4:00pm | daily trade review (duration unstated) | **not derivable** |

Two honest caveats: (a) "live session" overstates genuine price-watching, because the same accounts show much of the session spent in meetings, calls, models and RFQs; (b) the ranges cluster around **50–60% of the working day inside market hours, with roughly one quarter to one third of the day in pre-open preparation and 15–25% in post-close processing.** This is a small-sample, white-collar-desk picture, not a measured population statistic.

**A sharper version of the same point, for discretion.** If "live session" is narrowed to *actively trading* rather than merely being at the desk, the only desk that specifies this is SMB Capital, where "the most active trading occurs between 9:30 AM till 11 AM and in the last hour of trading" — i.e. **about 2 hours of active trading out of a 6.5-hour session, roughly 30%** ([Benzinga, 2009](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/benzinga-staff/2009/9/13/smb-capital-outlines-its-daily-trading-strategy)) [SECONDARY]. On a 9-hour working day that is roughly **20% of the day in active trading, ~35% in pre-open preparation, and the remainder in review, journaling, meetings and non-market work.**

# Q2. What fraction of time is spent looking at live prices vs thinking / planning / reviewing?

**Direct, credible measurement of "screen time vs thinking time" for professional discretionary traders does not appear to exist.** Every quantified figure I could verify is either (a) self-reported in an interview, (b) a vendor/administrator survey of a specific population, (c) about a proxy population (retail investors, allocators, heads of desk), or (d) an arithmetic derivation from a timetable. I have labelled each accordingly.

## 2.1 Hardest numbers found

### (1) Ray Dalio — "six to eight hours a day, six days a week, digging [into research]"
> "I usually wake up at about 6 a.m. I meditate. I have breakfast. Then I dive into a variety of things. **I check in on the markets, and then I spend six to eight hours a day, six days a week, digging [into research].** I love digging."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner, founder of Bridgewater] ([NBC/CNBC Make It](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/business/money-report/billionaire-ray-dalio-on-his-routine-free-life-what-keeps-him-up-at-night-and-his-next-chapter/2667996/))

This is the single most useful figure in the brief: for a macro investor running the largest hedge fund in the world, the market check is a brief morning item and **research is a 36–48 hour/week block**. Note the important self-contradiction in his own framing: he titles the section "On his lack of a daily routine: 'I go with the flow'" and says "I have no routines" — so this is a time *budget*, not a fixed timetable.

Bridgewater's own description of the *Daily Observations* confirms that written research, not screen-watching, is the firm's core daily artefact: the BDO "—or what we call 'the wire' internally—has been our way of giving our clients and readers a real-time view into how we're processing the world." Co-CIOs and senior investors "also share what the BDO means to them, its role in our investment process, and how the process of writing a BDO works today" [PRIMARY - institution] ([bridgewater.com](https://www.bridgewater.com/50-years-of-the-bridgewater-daily-observations)). The page gives the archive cadence (e.g. "January 15, 1978," "December 13, 1986," … "September 10, 2024") but **does not disclose how many hours the process consumes**.

### (2) The Tower Fund Services survey of emerging hedge fund managers — the clearest survey evidence on the research/non-research split
> "Based on a recent Tower survey of emerging managers that have been in business for under 5 years, areas that time is dedicated is fairly consistent with **50%-75% being dedicated to Investment Research/Market Research/Portfolio Management; 10%-25% being current investor relations; 0% – 25% spent on capital raising**; and a small amount of time dedicated to other firm matters… **In reality (table 2) managers spend less time on investment matters (column 3, 30-40% upper half) and more time on non-investment matters (column 2, 10-25% lower half) than they WANT.**"
> Also: "Though it has not been quantified, there is a correlation of reduction in performance relative to the increase in manager activities not directly related to investment decisions."
> — [SECONDARY - survey conducted by Tower Fund Services (a fund administrator) and reported by Hedge Fund Insight; the primary tables are images and the sample size is not stated in the text] ([hedgefundinsight.org](https://www.hedgefundinsight.org/emerging-hedge-fund-managers-time-balancing-act/))

**This is the single best answer to Q2's underlying question for a small fund**: the gap between the *desired* 50–75% on investment work and the *actual* 30–40% is the headline. Caveat: 2015 vintage, small self-selected sample, administrator-authored, and the "reality" figures are reported only as column maxima without N.

### (3) Head traders spend almost no time trading
> "Of **eight global head traders interviewed by The TRADE, half said trading takes up on average between 0% and 5% of their time on a weekly basis.**"
> "One global head trader commented that around **20-30% of his time** is taken up attending meetings on MiFID II and compliance, while another said MiFID II and EMIR has accounted for **between 35-40% of his time**. The majority of head traders surveyed by The TRADE said **around a quarter of their time** is spent on MiFID II."
> "Being a head trader now means keeping up with market structure changes and regulation, rather than with what is going on in the markets. This has meant a dramatic reduction in the amount of time devoted to trading."
> — [PRIMARY - in-depth interviews with eight named-role heads of trading, reported by trade press] ([thetradenews.com](https://www.thetradenews.com/head-of-trading-a-role-redefined/))

This is about heads of desk, not line traders — but it is the only *directly measured* "% of time at the market" figure I found for buy-side professionals, and it is startling: **0–5%**.

### (4) Greenwich Associates buy-side trading survey (n=256)
> "In Q4 2018, Greenwich Associates interviewed 256 buy-side traders across Europe and the United States working on equity, fixed-income or foreign exchange trading desks."
> "**More than half (54%) of traders and PMs said they spent more time trading electronically in 2018** than they did the previous year, with only 5% cutting back… Roughly **47% of respondents said they spent more time talking to compliance in 2018**, while **45% had to allocate more of their day reviewing transaction cost analysis (TCA) or other best-execution metrics**. Another **30% spent more time on 'desk oversight'**… roughly **55% of respondents said they spent less time on the phone with dealers in 2018**."
> — [PRIMARY research (Greenwich Associates), SECONDARY reporting (eFinancialCareers)] ([greenwich.com](https://www.greenwich.com/market-structure-technology/buy-side-spending-battle-compensation-vs-technology), [efinancialcareers.co.uk](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2019/02/how-traders-and-portfolio-managers-spend-their-time-in-2019))

Note these are **directional year-on-year changes, not absolute shares** — the survey establishes that compliance, TCA and oversight are *growing* as a share of the buy-side day, at the expense of dealer conversations, but it does not give a 60/40 or 80/20 split.

### (5) Vendor claim: profitable traders are active <3 hours/day
> "**Our average profitable trader is active less than 3 hours a day. Our average struggling trader logs more than 6 hours a day.**"
> And the prescription: "spend 90% of your time practicing conscious skill development in Simulation Mode… and 10% trading live."
> — [SECONDARY - vendor-reported statistics from a trading-education company (Hawkeye Traders); population, sample and method undisclosed. Could be genuine internal data or marketing. Use with caution.] ([hawkeyetraders.com](https://www.hawkeyetraders.com/why-trading-more-produces-less/))

### (6) Retail benchmark (not professional, but rigorous): the NBER browser-data study
> "The median individual investor spends approximately **six minutes** on research per trade on traded tickers, mostly just before the trade; the mean spends **around half an hour**."
> — [PRIMARY - academic working paper, NBER Working Paper No. 33625, Laarits & Wurgler, March 2025, browser data on ~484 individual investors] ([nber.org PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33625/w33625.pdf); summary by AAII [aaii.com](https://www.aaii.com/investor-update/article/299948-six-minutes-study-finds-that-investors-often-skip-proper-stock-research))

The AAII summary adds the behavioural detail that matters for Q2: "Snapshot pages (aka stock quote pages) and chart pages ranked at the top, with **93% of investors looking at them**," while "almost 40% of investors [sought] out some sort of earnings information," spending "just under two minutes on average" ([aaii.com](https://www.aaii.com/investor-update/article/299948-six-minutes-study-finds-that-investors-often-skip-proper-stock-research)). **This is the mirror image of the professional pattern: retail time is overwhelmingly price-page time; professional time is reported as overwhelmingly research time.** It is not evidence about professionals.

### (7) Steve Cohen — the market does not require continuous attention
> "But the markets are different today—**you don't necessarily have to sit in front of the screens as much anymore, because a lot of time during the day markets are just meandering around. Actually I think the most important part of the day is the last hour of the day.** But there's times during the day when things are quiet, and so you don't have to sit and watch, which is somewhat liberating."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner (Point72), in a Fortune interview] ([Fortune](http://fortune.com/2016/10/21/billionaire-steve-cohen-point72-interview/); quoted also at [hedgefundinsight.org](http://www.hedgefundinsight.org/quotation-of-the-day-from-steve-cohen-of-point72/))

### (8) Stanley Druckenmiller — long hours, but newspaper-first
> "I do this job because I just love it. **I'm 71 years old. I'm still in the office 12 hours a day.** I worked every day through [Covid-19]. I'm fascinated by it."
> "To keep himself informed in a technologically developing world, **Druckenmiller reads three to four newspapers every morning.**"
> Also: "I'm wrong at least 35 percent to 40 percent of the time."
> — [PRIMARY - named practitioner, reported from a live appearance] ([Bowdoin Orient](https://bowdoinorient.com/2024/04/19/druckenmiller-gives-advice-to-students-discusses-u-s-macroeconomic-trends/))

### (9) Large-fund PM: an 8-hour day, with the balance explicitly named
> "I work hard, but I don't work long hours. **On a typical day, I work for around eight hours** and make time for walks and for listening to music."
> "When I get to the office, the first thing I do is to check the pnl of my portfolio after the overnight session… **A lot of my time is then spent monitoring markets and reading research produced by banks to formulate new investment ideas.** I also check the scanners a lot… Throughout the day, I'm checking my risk levels and discussing the need to make changes, or not, with the senior PM."
> — [SECONDARY - anonymous first-person, published under a pseudonym by eFinancialCareers; cannot be independently attributed, but is unusually concrete about hours] ([efinancialcareers.co.uk](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/finance/portfolio-manager-job-hedge-fund-lifestyle))

### (10) Pre-open work is claimed to be where the edge is (prop/retail framing)
> "**Pre-market prep takes me about 1 hour and 45 minutes. That sounds like a lot for someone who only trades for two hours. But this block is where 80% of my edge comes from.** I'm not reacting to the market during the session. I'm executing a plan that I wrote before the bell."
> Routine: "7:30 AM Pre-Market Wake up… / 9:30 AM Trading Session Market opens. First 15 minutes: observe only. No trades… / 9:45 AM Execution window opens… Max 3-4 trades / 11:30 AM Wind-Down Stop taking new trades… / 12:15 PM Post-Session Review: did I follow the plan? / 1:00 PM Done."
> — [FOLKLORE - anonymous, unsourced single-author blog with no named practitioner or data. Structurally useful as an illustration of the 1h45m-prep-to-2h-trading ratio; do NOT treat the "80% of my edge" number as evidence.] ([proptradingvibes.com](https://proptradingvibes.com/blog/trading-routine-daily))

### (11) Coach claim on excess screen time
> "In fact, **spending 8, 10, or even 15 hours a day staring at charts could be hurting your trading.**… More screen time doesn't mean more profits."
> — [FOLKLORE - podcast episode description, no data cited] ([podtail - The Trading Coach Podcast ep. 1344](https://podtail.com/podcast/the-trading-coach-podcast/1344-how-much-screen-time-does-a-trader-really-nee/))

## 2.2 Qualitative but well-attested claims about the balance

Even where numbers are absent, several **primary** sources make the same directional claim: professional time is weighted toward preparation and review, and *away* from continuous price-watching.

- **Steenbarger:** "I have long maintained that one of the best ways of identifying successful traders is to observe what they are doing when they're not trading" [PRIMARY] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/03/best-practices-in-trading-organizing_2.html)).
- **Steenbarger on the failure mode of screen-bound routines:** "A great deal of trading skill comes from pattern recognition and openness to signals from the head and gut. **Many traders attempt to read markets while engaging in routines that effectively shut their eyes.** … This form of market preparation may be every bit as important as what we do to generate ideas and trades" [PRIMARY] ([traderfeed.blogspot.com, 2016](http://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2016/12/improving-your-trading-by-developing.html)). He identifies the three things that degrade real-time reading as "Fatigue," "Stress" and "Cognitive Inflexibility."
- **Market-maker desk reality:** "Long periods of monitoring, punctuated by short bursts requiring immediate decisions" [SECONDARY] ([tradermath.org](https://www.tradermath.org/courses/markets-trading-and-risk/the-daily-life-of-a-trader-at-firms-like-imc)).
- **SMB Capital's own empirical claim:** "When I crunch the data my win rate is significantly higher for the stocks that I pre plan versus breaking news or market plays that occur during the day" [PRIMARY] ([smbtraining.com](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/why-i-game-plan-every-day)). This is the mechanism by which preparation substitutes for screen time.
- **Mauboussin's tenth attribute of great investors** is literally *reading*: "**Read (and keep an open mind): Great investors generally allocate time to reading, read material across a wide spectrum of disciplines and read material they do not necessarily agree with.**" Source: "Thirty Years: Reflections on the Ten Attributes of Great Investors," Mauboussin, Callahan & Majd, Credit Suisse, August 4, 2016 — [PRIMARY - named practitioner/researcher] as summarised by AAII ([aaii.com](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/10-attributes-great-investors-share)); the essay's broader argument ("It is harder than ever to generate excess returns… the 'paradox of skill'") is reproduced at [acquirersmultiple.com](https://acquirersmultiple.com/2016/09/the-ten-attributes-of-great-fundamental-investors-michael-j-mauboussin/).
- **Howard Marks on where time *should* go:** "the big question is how much of your time, effort, and capital — and self-esteem — will you risk in the effort of trying to be right? And how much will you spend trying to avoid being wrong? They are two different things" [PRIMARY - named practitioner, Bloomberg TV transcript] ([businessinsider.com](https://www.businessinsider.com/howard-marks-dare-to-be-great-2014-4)). Note: this is a framing of *risk* allocation as much as time allocation; I found no Marks statement quantifying hours.
- **Morgan Housel's "99% of long-term investing is doing nothing":** "A pilot once described his job as 'hours and hours of boredom punctuated by moments of sheer terror.' I think of investing the same way. Your success as an investor will be determined by how you respond to punctuated moments of terror, not the years and years spent on cruise control" [SECONDARY - practitioner essay, journalist-published; about long-horizon investing, not the trading day] ([USA Today / Motley Fool](https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/personalfinance/2013/12/25/most-of-long-term-investing-is-doing-nothing/4189351/)).

| Bank trading desk, sell-side | "Eating lunch at the desk to avoid missing catalysts"; "Even during quieter moments, traders monitor P&L, order books, and market signals" | [SECONDARY - training provider] ([fe.training](https://www.fe.training/free-resources/careers-in-finance/day-in-the-life-of-trading-analyst/)) |

## 2.4 What the evidence actually supports (synthesis)

1. **For a senior macro/discretionary investor, "looking at live prices" appears to be a small slice of the day.** Dalio's own budget is the cleanest statement: a brief morning check-in on markets, then 6–8 hours/day of research ([NBC](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/business/money-report/billionaire-ray-dalio-on-his-routine-free-life-what-keeps-him-up-at-night-and-his-next-chapter/2667996/)). Cohen states the same conclusion from the trading side ([Fortune](http://fortune.com/2016/10/21/billionaire-steve-cohen-point72-interview/)).
2. **For a small fund, non-investment work is the biggest single competitor to research time** — the Tower survey's 50–75% *desired* vs 30–40% *actual* on investment matters is the most directly relevant number found ([hedgefundinsight.org](https://www.hedgefundinsight.org/emerging-hedge-fund-managers-time-balancing-act/)).
3. **For heads of trading desks, actual trading has collapsed to near zero (0–5% of the week)** while compliance/regulatory and desk oversight have expanded ([thetradenews.com](https://www.thetradenews.com/head-of-trading-a-role-redefined/)).
4. **For market makers and execution traders, the day is ~50–60% inside market hours but dominated by passive monitoring punctuated by bursts** ([tradermath.org](https://www.tradermath.org/courses/markets-trading-and-risk/the-daily-life-of-a-trader-at-firms-like-imc); derived from [efinancialcareers.co.uk](https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2022/06/citadel-securities-trading-job)).
5. **No one publishes a measured "hours staring at prices" statistic for professional discretionary traders.** The most-cited numbers in that space (e.g. "<3 hours active" vs ">6 hours struggling") come from a vendor with no disclosed methodology, and should be treated as marketing until replicated.

# WHAT I COULD NOT FIND

**1. No direct measurement of screen time vs thinking time for professional discretionary traders.**
This is the core gap. There is no time-and-motion study, no diary study, and no survey instrument I could locate that asks professional discretionary traders or PMs to split their day into "watching live prices," "research/analysis," "planning," and "review." Every figure in Section 2 is either self-reported in a media interview, a proxy population, or my own arithmetic.

**2. Top Traders Unplugged yielded almost nothing on daily routine.**
I downloaded and full-text-extracted two transcripts — [Episode #107 with Jean-Jacques Duhot](http://pw.toptradersunplugged.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/TopTradersUnplugged-transcript-ep107-Jean-Jacques-Duhot.pdf) and [Top Traders Round Table Episode #07 with Richard Dennis, Brian Proctor and Jerry Parker](http://pw.toptradersunplugged.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/ttrt-transcript-ep07.pdf). Both are ~13–20 pages of substantive process discussion (position sizing, breakout models, money management, robustness of research), but **neither contains an hour-by-hour account of a trading day**. The only routine-adjacent passage is Duhot on mindset: "it's all about your mindset, and your mindset will start by some routine and those daily routines I think help you to stay cool-minded in moments where you're getting challenged." The show's guest roster skews systematic/CTA, which likely explains the absence. I could not retrieve a discretionary-macro TTU episode with a day-in-the-life segment.

**3. Internal weekly/monthly strategy-review cadence at small funds is undocumented.**
I found the *allocator-facing* reporting cadence (monthly pack within days of month-end, quarterly attribution pack) from a fund administrator's content page ([cv5capital.io](https://www.cv5capital.io/insights/hedge-fund-investor-reporting-requirements)) — but nothing on how often a small fund actually holds a strategy review, who attends, how long it takes, or how it differs from the monthly letter. All weekly/monthly figures at the retail/prop end were unsourced SEO content.

**4. Prop-firm (FTMO / Topstep / Earn2Trade) content is generic and gives no time allocations.**
Topstep's routine article is a checklist with zero durations: it tells traders to do "morning prep and pre-market research," "review your plan," and "end the day with journaling" ([topstep.com](https://www.topstep.com/blog/daily-trading-routine-checklist)) [FOLKLORE/SEO]. Several FTMO, Topstep and Earn2Trade pages I attempted returned no usable content or failed to load. The one prop-desk account with real times was T3 Trading via Benzinga, and it gives times for the morning only ([Benzinga](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/general/education/14/05/4531192/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-prop-trader)).

**5. Market-maker "day in the life" pages from Jane Street and SIG have no timetables.**
[SIG's trading careers page](https://sig.com/careers/trading/) describes the role and links a "Day in the life of a Trading Intern" **video** whose content is not in the page text, with no schedule. I could not retrieve a Jane Street or IMC hour-by-hour written account; the Optiver account ([optiver.com](https://optiver.com/working-at-optiver/career-hub/the-internship-journey-to-becoming-a-trader/)) is the best market-maker first-person source I obtained, and it names the morning meeting and the post-close "reflection" but gives no clock times.

**6. Soros: no verifiable hour-by-hour account retrieved.**
I searched for accounts of George Soros's working day and found only summaries of *The Alchemy of Finance* and secondary profiles — nothing I could fetch that documents his daily timetable with attribution. I have therefore made no Soros claim in this brief.

**7. Paul Tudor Jones: only fragmentary timing.**
The best I could verify is a secondhand documentary summary: "Jones wants to catch volatility in other markets, so he'll wake up at 4 a.m. to trade gold in Hong Kong, or trade forex on a Sunday in New Zealand" [SECONDARY - journalism summarising a documentary] ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/paul-tudor-jones-trader-documentary-2011-10)). *Trader: The Documentary* itself is out of circulation and I could not fetch a transcript.

**8. Howard Marks's memos are not fetchable in a citable form.**
The Oaktree memo pages are JavaScript-rendered; "You Can't Predict. You Can Prepare." returned only navigation and disclaimers ([oaktreecapital.com](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/you-cant-predict-you-can-prepare)), and the 23 MB complete-collection PDF failed text extraction. I used a Bloomberg TV transcript republished by Business Insider instead, and I have **not** attributed any hour-based claim to Marks.

**9. Mauboussin's original essay PDF was not directly retrievable.**
The Credit Suisse paper ("Thirty Years: Reflections on the Ten Attributes of Great Investors," 2016) is no longer at its original URL; the Morgan Stanley successor URL 404s. I relied on the AAII summary and a reproduction of the essay's text, and have flagged the "reading" attribute accordingly. The essay contains **no** hour or percentage figures for reading time.

**10. No `[PRIMARY]` source quantifies what a discretionary trader does between 12pm and 2pm, or how much of the session is genuinely idle.** The closest are (a) the SMB Capital 2009 account, which says the 11am–3pm block is deliberately reallocated to review, journaling, a mid-day meeting and preparation for the last hour, with active trading concentrated in 9:30–11:00 and the final hour ([Benzinga](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/benzinga-staff/2009/9/13/smb-capital-outlines-its-daily-trading-strategy)) — one desk, one era, one strategy style — and (b) the Citadel Securities ADR trader's "11.30am. European markets close and there's usually a lull," which is one desk in one market microstructure. Neither is generalisable. I found **no** quantified figure for idle time on a discretionary macro or long/short equity book.

**11. One widely-circulated "day in the life" is explicitly not evidence.** Business Insider's "chaotic 18-hour day in the life of a Wall Street hedge fund analyst" is written by a fintech founder and labelled in its own text as "a somewhat farcical day in the life of a hedge fund manager based loosely on experiences from my colleagues, friends, friends of friends, and podcasts" ([businessinsider.com](https://www.businessinsider.com/hedgefund-analyst-wall-street-18-hour-day-in-the-life-2022-8)). It is a composite/satirical sketch. I have therefore cited no figure from it. It was fetched and reviewed, and is listed here only so the caller does not mistake it for a first-person account.
